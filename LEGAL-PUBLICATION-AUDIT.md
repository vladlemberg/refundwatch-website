### Deletion order and failure cases

1. Reauthenticate with the account's provider. Cancel/failure does not authorize local erasure. Record the deletion request locally.
2. Normal Apple flow revokes the app authorization before backend deletion. A failed Apple revocation stops the normal server call; local account data remains. This is not deletion of the user's Apple account.
3. Backend leases/blocks bank mutations, revokes known saved and pending Plaid credentials, and records progress. If a later Item fails, earlier revocations are not undone; retry skips recorded completions. Unknown unjournaled credentials are counted/referenced, not magically revoked.
4. Backend removes owner connection/journal records in batches and installs the deletion fence, then deletes Firebase Auth user. Auth deletion failure leaves the fence in place and retry only repeats the remaining step. No transaction spans Plaid, Firestore and Firebase Auth.
5. Lost-response recovery requires an authoritative `userNotFound` check. A network/App Check failure is not proof that remote deletion succeeded; local data is retained.
6. After server confirmation, local account namespace erasure is attempted. `finishConfirmedDeletion()` uses fallible operations and can sign out/report deleted while local erasure failed. A persisted confirmed ledger allows later launch cleanup; simultaneous ledger/erase failure is a further recovery limitation. Therefore no policy may promise immediate or atomic removal of every local copy.
7. Google SDK disconnect is conditional on the in-memory `usesGoogle` flag and uses `try?`; errors are ignored. Interrupted/restarted paths do not prove Google grant revocation. Sign-out and Firebase deletion do not mean the Google account or every Google-held copy is deleted.
8. Other-device cleanup is opportunistic: a restored session must successfully verify the Firebase user is missing. Offline devices and backups remain outside immediate control.
